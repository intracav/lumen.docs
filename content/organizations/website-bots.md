{{< tier-badge "max" >}} Most website chatbots are a liability in a lab coat: they improvise, they flatter, and on a medical site an improvised answer is a risk you can't take. A Lumen **Website Bot** is built on the opposite premise — it answers **only from the Knowledge Packs you assign it**, and when it can't ground an answer in your content, it says so.

## Grounded answers, with receipts

Every answer the bot gives is retrieved from your packs — your product documentation, your clinical evidence library, your FAQ — and rendered **with citations that link to the source, anchored to the right page**. A visitor who asks about your device's securement evidence doesn't get chatbot prose; they get an answer with a link into the actual document, at the actual page.

And when a visitor asks something your content doesn't cover, the bot deflects honestly — it doesn't guess, and it doesn't pretend. On a medical site, "I don't have documentation on that — here's how to reach us" is a *feature*. It's the difference between a rep who says "let me check" and one who makes something up at the booth.

You control the corpus per bot, down to individual documents — and publishing a document to a public-facing bot always requires explicit confirmation, so nothing internal leaks into the widget by accident.

## Lead capture with CRM sync

When a conversation turns commercial — a clinician asking about evaluation units, a materials manager asking about compatibility — the bot can capture the visitor's details as a lead. Leads land in an inbox your team works from (stages, assignees, notes) and sync to your CRM: **Zoho and HubSpot** are supported, with the ability to re-push if a sync hiccups. Lead alerts and digests keep the right person informed without anyone refreshing a dashboard.

## Conversation intelligence

The conversations themselves are the most underused asset a website has. The intelligence dashboard turns them into answers to questions you actually have:

- **What are visitors asking?** Real questions, aggregated by topic and product — with drill-down to the verbatim message and full transcript when an aggregate needs context.
- **Who's showing buying signals?** An intent-to-lead funnel, surfaced buying signals, and objections — so marketing sees what's working and sales sees what's stalling.
- **Where are the knowledge gaps?** Questions the bot couldn't ground are your content roadmap. If thirty visitors this month asked something your site doesn't answer, you now know exactly what to write next.

Sentiment and pipeline status round out the picture. It's post-market surveillance for your website: the signal was always there, this makes it legible.

## Multiple bots, one organization

An organization can run **multiple bots** — one per product line, one for a clinician audience and another for procurement, each with its own pack assignment, its own embed, and its own intelligence view. They share your org's governance and your team's access.

## Safety rails

Public-facing AI needs an engineering posture, not just a prompt:

- **Origin allowlists** — each bot's key only works from the domains you register. Copied embed code on someone else's site gets nothing.
- **Daily budgets** — per-bot daily message quotas cap worst-case usage, so a scripted abuse loop degrades gracefully instead of running your account hot.
- **Session-scoped access** — the widget operates on short-lived sessions with a publisher key, never on a member's credentials.
- **Test before you ship** — fire test questions at the bot's corpus from the admin side and read exactly what it would say, before a visitor does.
