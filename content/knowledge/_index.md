Lumen answers clinical questions using its own tools, such as drug databases, guidelines and PubMed. It can also answer from **your** material: a unit policy, a device's instructions for use, a paper you're reading or a note you wrote. When it does, the answer cites the document so you can open it and check.

## Which one should I use?

| You want to | Use |
|---|---|
| Ask about one file right now | Attach it to your message. See [Attachments](/chat/attachments/). |
| Have Lumen find and cite a file in future chats | Upload it to [Documents](/knowledge/documents/). |
| Make a policy citable for your whole department | Put it in a shared [folder](/knowledge/folders/), or ask an admin to add it to a team [Knowledge Pack](/knowledge/knowledge-packs/). |
| Use published guidelines or a device maker's documents | Switch on a [Knowledge Pack](/knowledge/knowledge-packs/). |
| Have Lumen remember your role, preferences or your own notes | [Memory and notes](/knowledge/second-brain/). |
| See what a website assistant or department answers from | The [Knowledge Map](/knowledge/knowledge-map/) (admins). |

## Three words worth knowing

- **Searchable**, **Processing**, **Couldn't process**: whether Lumen has finished reading a source and can search it.
- **Always-on guidance**: short instructions an admin gives Lumen for every answer in a scope, such as "Our hospital uses the term midline, not extended-dwell PIV". It's always applied, never searched or cited.
- **Available isn't approved**: a document being searchable doesn't mean anyone has verified or approved it. Those are separate steps.

{{< callout type="safety" title="Check what Lumen cites" >}}
Lumen cites your documents so you can check them. A citation shows where a statement came from, not that the source is current or correct. Open it before you act.
