Education without documentation is a story; education *with* documentation is a competency record. Lumen's quiz tool handles both halves: it builds certification-quality quizzes from whatever content you point it at, scores your attempts, and files every scored attempt in your **Cert Binder** — with a downloadable proof-of-completion PDF for each one.

## Building a quiz from anything

Ask Lumen for a quiz in plain language and point it at the material — a guideline, a document from your library, a [Knowledge Pack](/knowledge/knowledge-packs/), or just a topic:

{{< chat-demo title="From guideline to quiz" >}}
{{< user >}}Build a 10-question quiz on our new CLABSI prevention bundle for the unit's annual competency day. Passing score 80%.{{< /user >}}
{{< lumen >}}Here's your quiz: *CLABSI Prevention Bundle — Competency Check*. 10 questions across insertion technique, maintenance, and surveillance, mixed difficulty, 80% to pass. Open it to begin — your attempt will be scored and saved to your Cert Binder.{{< /lumen >}}
{{< /chat-demo >}}

Under the hood the tool works the way a good educator does: it **plans** first — analyzing the material to decide how many questions it supports, the right mix of formats, and how to spread them across topics and cognitive levels — then **creates** the quiz as an interactive artifact you take right in Lumen, and finally **scores** your answers with per-question rationale and a topic-by-topic breakdown, so a miss tells you *what to re-read*, not just that you missed.

Every question carries an explanation of why the correct answer is correct — the quiz teaches on the way through, not just at the end. The pass mark defaults to 70% and can be set wherever your program needs it.

For ready-made framing, two [Skills](/skills-and-specialists/skills/) pair naturally with quizzes: **Clinical Quiz Builder** (interactive quizzes with evidence citations behind every answer) and **Study Guide** (retention-organized material to study *before* the quiz).

## The Cert Binder: your attempt history

Every scored attempt is saved automatically — no "remember to screenshot your result." The Cert Binder is the running record: which quizzes you've taken, each attempt's score and pass/fail verdict, and how many attempts it took. Retakes are stored as separate numbered attempts, so improvement is visible rather than overwritten.

## Proof-of-completion PDFs

Each attempt can be downloaded as a **proof-of-completion PDF** — your name, the quiz, the score, and the date — the artifact your educator, manager, or credentialing file actually wants. One attempt, one certificate; a retake that clears the bar generates its own.

{{< callout type="info" >}}
**Attempts belong to the person who took them.** Your Cert Binder is yours — a record of your own attempts, downloadable by you. That maps cleanly onto how competency files work: each clinician holds their own documentation.
{{< /callout >}}

## Where teams use it

- **Unit education** — turn a new or revised policy into a short quiz for the next staff meeting; everyone leaves with a scored attempt on file.
- **Competency documentation** — annual skills days generate the paperwork as a side effect: take the quiz, download the PDF, done.
- **Journal club** — quiz the group on this month's paper; the score report's per-question rationale doubles as the discussion agenda.
- **Precepting and onboarding** — a new hire works through the unit's core protocols one quiz at a time, and the Cert Binder becomes their orientation record.
